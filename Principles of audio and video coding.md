### Video compression algorithm

#### Spatial redundancy

In a frame, a large area of ​​pixels are the same, such as a red photo. The information of one pixel can be used to represent this area of ​​pixels

#### Temporal redundancy

In a video, a piece or a point of pixels in the same position has not changed, such as a static picture, the pixels in the same position during this period can be represented by a sample

### Implementation of video coding technology

Video is formed by the continuous playback of different frames

#### I frame B frame P frame

#### I frame👇

I frame: It is an independent frame with all information, the most complete picture, and can be decoded independently without referring to other images. The first frame in a video sequence is always an I frame

#### P frame👇

P frame: It needs to refer to different parts of the previous I frame or P frame to encode. P frame is dependent on the previous P or I frame. For example, if only part of the pixels of the picture have changed, you can refer to the unchanged part of the previous frame

#### B frame👇

B frame: "Bidirectional predictive coding frame". It can refer not only to the previous frame but also to the next frame. However, because it refers to the next frame, it is not suitable for real-time transmission (for example: video conferencing)

#### Bitrate

Bitrate refers to the data flow used by the video file per unit time, also called bitrate, which is the most important part of picture quality control in video encoding. Under the same resolution, the larger the bitrate of the video file, the smaller the compression ratio and the better the picture quality

#### Bitrate control: VBR & CBR

Pre-knowledge: Taking a video with 25 frames per second as an example, a GOP group is defined in 25 frames of images. A GOP group is a "small group" in a video, based on an I frame. The size of the video is mainly related to the number of I frames. The more I frames, the larger the video. When the compression method of video encoding is the same and the clarity requirements are the same, the longer the GOP group length, the fewer the number of groups, the fewer I frames, and the smaller the video size

There are two ways to control the bitrate: VBR and CBR

VBR dynamically changes the length of the GOP group through an algorithm. When there are many moving images, the group length is shortened, the number of groups is increased, and the number of I frames is increased, and the picture is clearer

CBR controls the size of the bitrate to remain unchanged as much as possible, with the purpose of stabilizing the bandwidth.
